================================================================================
                           3M 301 MAINTENANCE PERSON
================================================================================

Purpose of 3M System: Provides maintenance and material managers throughout the
Navy with processes and tools for planning, acquiring, organizing, directing,
controlling, and evaluating the manpower and material resources used to support
maintenance.

The goal of the Ship's 3M Program is to manage maintenance support to achieve
maximum equipment operational readiness.

PRIMARY 3M INSTRUCTIONS
-----------------------
1. NAVSEAINST 4790.8 Series: Also known as the Ship's 3M Manual, provides a
   detailed explanation of 3M components including MIPs and MRCs as well as
   processes such as submitting feedback reports and equipment configuration
   changes (OPNAV 4790/CK).
2. COMUSFLTFORCOMINST 4790.3 Volume VI Chap 19: Also known as the JFMM, provides
   a list of the responsibilities at each level of the Chain of Command,
   instructions for management of the 3M program and inspection procedures for
   all commands separated by TYCOM. (Chapter 19-6 for Naval Surface Forces)
3. COMNAVSURFPAC/COMNAVSURFLANTINST 4790.1 Series: Outlines the process by which
   surface forces certify their 3M programs. Outlines the functions of the 3M
   365 program and explains how each 3M pillar is scored.

SUPPLEMENTAL 3M INSTRUCTIONS
----------------------------
1. OPNAVINST 5100.19 Series: Navy Safety and Occupational Health for Forces
   Afloat, provides safety instructions for all afloat forces. Directly
   referenced in every MRC onboard.
2. OPNAV M-5100.23: (Formerly OPNAVINST 5100.23 Series) Navy Safety and
   Occupational Health Manual, directs the construction and administration of
   safety programs across the Navy. The defining safety instruction for all
   shore-based activities.
3. NSTM 670: (Formerly OPNAVINST 5100.28 Series) Hazardous Material User's
   Guide, or HMUG. Provides handling, PPE, and emergency responses for hazmat,
   separated by hazard group. Guidance is generalized and does not override
   protective requirements listed on MSDS; when instructions differ, use the
   highest level of protection recommended.
4. NAVSEA Tech Pub - Tagout Users Manual: Also known as TUM, provides direction
   for tag-out process and outlines responsibilities at all levels.

COMPONENTS OF 3M SYSTEM
-----------------------
1. PMS: Planned Maintenance System is used to maintain equipment within design
   specifications through preventive maintenance and to identify and correct
   potential problems before the equipment or system becomes inoperable.
2. MDS: Maintenance Data System provides information and reports for fleet
   maintenance and maintenance support. Includes the Current Ship's Maintenance
   Project (CSMP) and gathers maintenance history, which is stored in the Ship's
   3M System Database.

CHAIN OF COMMAND ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES
-----------------------------------------
NOTE: This is a simplified list of responsibilities and is NOT all-inclusive.
Detailed information is available in JFMM Vol VI Chap 19-6.2.

- Commanding Officer: responsible for establishing an aggressive Self-Assessment
  program that validates the administration and execution of maintenance and
  detects improperly conducted maintenance or falsely reported accomplishments.
- Executive Officer: The Command's 3M Manager, responsible to CO for the
  management of the 3M program. Tasked with ensuring the effectiveness of the 3M
  Self-Assessment program, final review of any shifts of maintenance
  responsibility between departments, and ensuring personnel receive adequate
  and effective 3M training. Required to approve all split MIPs.
- 3M Coordinator: The 3MC serves as 3M Assistant to the XO. Serves as the ship's
  technical expert in the 3M program, advising the chain of command to the
  current status of the program and managing day-to-day operation. Responsible
  for reviewing and maintaining the CSMP, overseeing all updates to the SKED
  program, managing 3M PQS qualification, and maintaining a log of all changes,
  directives, and notices related to all work centers carried onboard, as well
  as distributing information to all work centers.
- Department Head: Responsible to the XO for the operation and execution of 3M
  within the department. Tasked with review and approval for all 3M alerts and
  LEM status, digitally signing all FBRs within 5 days of origin, designating
  all 3M personnel in writing, and maintaining a self-assessment program within
  the department. Reports all changes in status of 3M to the XO.
- Department 3M Assistant: Serves to assist the 3MC in coordinating and
  supervising administration of the 3M program for their department. Offers
  assistance and direction to WCS within their department and advises the DH on
  the status of departmental 3M.
- Division Officer: Responsible to the DH for execution of maintenance within
  the division. Required to review and approve weekly close out on the first
  workday of each week, conduct 3M spot checks, review divisional work
  candidates and approve parts on order, and ensure 3M documents are submitted
  to DH in a timely manner. Also required to review and approve FBRs within 3
  days of origin.
- Leading Chief Petty Officer: Responsible for ensuring all division personnel
  are properly trained and equipped to conduct maintenance and WCS are
  knowledgeable of their maintenance requirements. Also responsible for
  approving all lineouts and approving work completed by outside authorities.
  Initiates Work Center revisions and may approve weekly close out in absence of
  DIVO.
- Work Center Supervisor: Responsible to the DIVO and LCPO for scheduling and
  assigning maintenance within the Work Center, maintaining an accurate and
  current LOEP, maintaining a 13-week accountability log, keeping the CSMP and
  configuration data for the Work Center current, submitting FBRs any time MRCs
  are unclear or contain errors, reviewing Forecasting Report to ensure tools,
  parts, materials and test equipment are available for maintenance tasks, and
  ensuring Work Center personnel are appropriately trained and qualified to
  perform all required maintenance.
- Repair Parts Petty Officer: Interfaces with Supply personnel to acquire
  required tools, materials, and repair parts in support of corrective and
  preventive maintenance. Responsible to WCS and LCPO for tracking outstanding
  requisitions and turn-in for depot level repair (DR) items as well as
  inventory of any Maintenance Assist Modules (MAMs).
- Maintenance Person: Responsible to WCS to perform any assigned maintenance,
  report completion of maintenance, and inform the WCS of any questions or
  discrepancies regarding assigned maintenance or the status of equipment in the
  Work Center.

KEY TERMS
---------
List Of Effective Pages (LOEP): A listing of Maintenance Index Pages (MIP) for
systems and equipment assigned to each work center, those systems and equipment
not requiring PMS, or those systems and equipment currently being evaluated for
PMS. Also known as PMS-5 Report.

Maintenance Index Page (MIP): A complete index of Maintenance Requirement Cards
(MRC) applicable to a ship system, subsystem, or equipment.

Maintenance Requirement Card (MRC): Provides detailed procedures for performing
maintenance requirements and describes who, what, when, how, and with what
resources a specific maintenance requirement will be accomplished.

PERIODICITY CODES
-----------------
Periodicity Codes: Identify the interval in which a maintenance task must be
performed. There are three categories of periodicity: calendar, non-calendar,
and IEM.

Calendar Periodicity Codes: Start with a letter representing the period
designated between performance requirements:
  - D = Daily
  - W = Weekly
  - M = Monthly
  - Q = Quarterly
  - S = Semi-Annually
  - A = Annually

A number preceding the letter represents a number of days/weeks/months in the
period, i.e. 2D = Every two days and 36M = Every 36 months. Numbers do not
appear for Q, S, or A periodicities.

Non-Calendar Periodicity Codes: Maintenance performed for a specific event
instead of a period of time.
  - R = Situational Requirement (Getting underway, prior to each use)
  - U = Unscheduled Maintenance (Directed by another MRC from a specific
    condition or used to recover from equipment casualty)
  - AP = Assessment Procedure (directed by an outside activity like INSURV or
    ISEA)

Inactive Equipment Maintenance (IEM) Codes: Maintenance related to items placed
in an IEM status.
  - LU = Lay-Up - Placing in IEM
  - PM = Periodic Maintenance - Maintenance required to be performed after
    equipment has been in IEM for a designated period of time
  - SU = Start-Up - Removing from IEM
  - OT = Operational Test - Intended to ensure equipment is operational after
    completing IEM status.

Periodicity Code Numbering: The periodicity code also includes a number for
specific identification. MRCs are typically numbered consecutively, e.g. "M-1",
"M-2", "M-3", etc. Sometimes MRCs will be added or removed over the life of a
MIP so the sequence is no longer in direct sequential order. Non-sequential
numbers will not affect scheduling or management control.

SYSCOM MRC CONTROL NUMBER
-------------------------
A 7-character alphanumeric code that identifies a specific version of an MRC. It
is made up of three parts:

  Example:  81  9NUC  N
            |    |    |
            |    |    +-- Parts Required
            |    +------- Library Control Number
            +------------ Date Code

Date Code: Two characters that represent the last time the MRC was updated.
First character value 1-9/A/B/C for the month; 1=January, 9=September,
A=October, B=November, C=December. Second character is the last digit of the
year; 8=2018. All MRCs are rewritten at least once every 10 years in order to
maintain the system. So: A date code of 21 would apply to an MRC written or
revised in February 2021. A date code of C7 would be an MRC that hasn't been
changed since December 2017. MIPs also have a date code, after the 7-digit MIP
number (e.g. 4371/003-31).

Library Control Number: A 4-character code that is a unique identifier for that
MRC. If evidence shows an annual check needs to be done semiannually, the A-5
may be changed to an S-3, depending on how many MRCs are already in the MIP with
those numbers. But the index number will not change until that MRC is retired or
massively rewritten.

Parts Required: A single letter code that is important to planning maintenance.
An "N" means no repair parts are required for completion of the check. A "Y"
means that there are parts that must be acquired prior to performing the
maintenance. Since work centers are not permitted to stockpile spare parts, the
"Y" code is required by supply to justify ordering parts for equipment that has
not yet failed. There is also a "U" code seen on "U" periodicity MRCs. These
MRCs can also justify ordering parts but since they aren't scheduled the parts
can only be ordered when the trigger event for the "U" check has occurred.

RATES
-----
Rates: The rate listed on an MRC identifies the recommended skill level of the
person(s) capable of performing the maintenance requirement and the number of
persons required to perform the maintenance requirement. Qualified personnel
other than the rate or rank specified may be assigned. Substitution of other
personnel is NOT allowed when an NEC code, DCPO or Safety PO is assigned.

TOOLS, PARTS, MATERIALS, TEST EQUIPMENT (TPME)
----------------------------------------------
All items are identified by Standard PMS Item Name (SPIN) number in brackets.
The SPIN is a number assigned to TPMTE that can be referenced against the
Standard PMS Materials Identification Guide (SPMIG) to identify NSN for
ordering. TPMTE is separated into five basic categories:
1. Test Equipment. Only used for General Purpose Portable Electrical and
   Electronic Test Equipment (GPETE). Should be listed by subcategory (SCAT)
   code which identifies a group of appropriate test equipment models. Approved
   models can be found by searching for SCAT code on the Test, Measurement, and
   Diagnostic Equipment Index (TMDE-I). Each command has a designated inventory
   for PETE based upon its maintenance requirements.
2. Materials. Include a wide range of administrative supplies and housekeeping
   items which may or may not be entirely consumed. Examples include paper or
   hazmat which may be consumed in performance of the MRC or buckets, ladders
   and funnels which are not.
3. Parts. Any items which are an integral component of the equipment. This
   includes items such as gaskets, light bulbs and filters. Items in this
   category will be found listed in the APL/AEL.
4. Tools. All common hand tools with the exception of "special tools" unique to
   the equipment being worked on. Includes hammers, wrenches, screwdrivers, etc.
5. Miscellaneous. Covers all items not otherwise covered under the other four
   categories. This will include tools unique to the equipment such as a custom
   spacer tool or special interface cable, as well as maintenance laptops and
   instructions related to the MRC. This includes items that appear on an APL
   but are not normally installed in equipment during operation.

VALIDATING AN MRC
-----------------
To ensure you are using the correct, current MRC:
1. Compare the SYSCOM MRC number printed on the MRC to the number printed on the
   MIP. Ensure the date code matches on both.
2. Compare the full SYSCOM MIP number on the MIP to the LOEP. Ensure the date
   code matches on both. (NOTE: The LOEP is not printed in paperless
   configurations like onboard BAINBRIDGE. The MIP loaded in SKED will always be
   the most recent and correct version available onboard.)
3. Compare the FR number and date of change on LOEP to the Change Page in the
   PMS Manual. Ensure dates match. (Again, this does not apply in paperless
   configurations like BAINBRIDGE.)

FEEDBACK REPORTS (FBR)
----------------------
Used to report problems with PMS and also to request PMS coverage for newly
installed systems or equipment. Generated inside SKED and then exported by the
3MC for submission to Naval Sea Logistics Center (NSLC) for processing. Required
to be approved and released by the command within 7 days of creation. There are
two types of FBR:
1. CAT A (Non-Technical): Intended to meet PMS needs which do not require
   technical review, including changes to Work Center LOEPs. Also submitted to
   request classified PMS documents which cannot be generated locally.
2. CAT B (Technical): Used to report technical discrepancies inhibiting
   performance of PMS. Classified into Routine or Urgent.
  a. Routine: An FBR that is not related to safety of the ship or personnel.
     Used to report technical discrepancies or request clarification of MRC
     procedures. Response is due from Technical Review Activity within 21 days.
     This is the most common type of FBR.
  b. Urgent: Involves the safety of personnel, safety of ship, or potential for
     equipment damage if the MRC is followed as written. Urgent FBRs are
     forwarded to NSLC, the cognizant SYSCOM, BUMED, NAVSAFECEN and TYCOM by
     naval message including the PMS FBR Serial Number. The message must
     describe the unsafe procedure or conditions as well as the MIP/MRC
     involved. SYSCOM or BUMED is required to respond within one working day of
     receipt and the response is distributed to all commands carrying the MIP
     via all TYCOMS.

INACTIVE EQUIPMENT MAINTENANCE (IEM)
------------------------------------
A state for equipment that will not be in use for a specific period of time in
which maintenance is "hibernated". NOTE: Any maintenance that would be scheduled
during an IEM period is REQUIRED to be completed upon exiting the IEM state.
1. Type I IEM: Equipment that will:
  a. Remain onboard
  b. Is not scheduled for corrective maintenance or overhaul, or
  c. Will be inactive during extended shutdown periods and maintenance
     availabilities as determined by the Department Head.
2. Type II IEM: Equipment that is inactive and:
  a. Subject to corrective maintenance, overhaul, or removal for safe storage,
  b. Temporarily transferred to another activity for maintenance responsibility
     for extended shutdown periods as determined by the Department Head.

Certain PMS is still required on equipment while in IEM to ensure continued
viability: These will be found with PM periodicity codes. Any LU, SU, and OT
MRCs that apply to the equipment will also be required when entering or exiting
an IEM status. IEM status is solely at the discretion of the Department Head and
requires their signature to initiate or remove.

TAG-OUT
-------
Used to secure valves, electric circuits, and other controls to prevent risk to
personnel or equipment while maintenance is being performed. Tags come in two
forms:
1. CAUTION Tag: Yellow in color, provides specific instructions for when and how
   equipment is permitted to be used.
2. DANGER Tag: Red in color, equipment is not to be energized or activated in
   any way while tag is present.

Tags can only be hung for up to 24 hours when tag-out is conducted for
preventive maintenance. Tag-out for corrective maintenance or IEM can remain
until the situation that caused the IEM or CM is resolved. When a tag is
hanging, it carries the authority of an order from the Commanding Officer. Any
tag that is found to be damaged or removed from its hung position should be
immediately reported to the authorizing officer and the Department Head, and
work on the affected equipment must stop immediately until the equipment status
can be identified and tags re-hung.

The tag-out process for PMS is as follows:
1. Maintenance person identifies the item required for tag-out and requests
   tag-out in the eTagout program. The reason is the MIP/MRC and the applicable
   documentation is any available drawings that support identification of that
   item as the one that will isolate the equipment to be worked on.
2. A second person logs into eTagout to verify the item is correctly identified
   based on the applicable documentation.
  a. If there is a Repair Activity involved (common in shipyard/availability
     periods) they also have to verify documentation.
3. Maintenance person goes to the applicable authorizing officer (CSOOW or EOOW)
   and presents the applicable documentation to justify the tag-out and prove it
   will properly isolate the equipment.
4. If Authorizing Officer (AO) agrees with documentation and tag position, they
   will generate tag label and Tag Hanging Sheet (THS).
5. Qualified maintenance person takes tag and THS, locates the
   breaker/valve/control, places it into the tag-out position (secured/off/etc)
   and hangs the tag on the breaker/valve/control itself.
6. Maintenance person then signs the tag and THS and returns to AO.
7. Second qualified person takes the THS and locates the tag, independently
   verifying it is hanging in the correct place and equipment is in the correct
   position. Second person does not have to be the same one that verified
   documentation unless directed by local instruction.
8. Second person signs the tag and the THS, leaving the tag hanging in position.
   They then return THS to the AO.
  a. If Repair Activity is involved, an additional qualified RA representative
     will have to verify tags.
9. When AO accepts tags are hung and files THS as an active tag-out, work may
   begin.

After work is complete, the following process will be used to clear tags:
1. Responsible Work Center representative will log in to eTagout and mark "Work
   Complete" for tags ready to be cleared.
  a. If Repair Activity is involved, RA representative also must verify "Work
     Complete" before tag-out can be cleared.
2. Authorizing Officer will acknowledge work complete and print Tag Removal
   Sheet (TRS).
3. Maintenance person takes TRS, locates tag, removes it and restores the
   breaker/valve/control/etc to normal operating position, and then signs TRS
   with time the tag was removed.
4. Maintenance person returns TRS and tag to AO, and then destroys the tag in
   the presence of AO.
5. AO files TRS with Line Item Record Sheet (LIRS) as a cleared tag-out.

If THS or TRS is damaged or fails to print, AO can generate a Line Item Record
Sheet (LIRS) to function in place of THS/TRS.

For more information, the eTagout program has a "Help" tab available.

AWN
---
AWN: Automated Work Notification is the website currently in use as USS
BAINBRIDGE's Maintenance Data System, or MDS. It is accessible through the MFOM
Afloat Toolbox for Maintenance, found at:
  https://mfom.ddg96.navy.mil/ATM/ConsentBanner.aspx
Through this locally hosted web platform, personnel can perform common MDS
tasks:

Equipment Configuration Alteration: Also known as OPNAV 4790/CK. Most commonly
accessed through Equipment Validations, this tool is used to alter, correct, and
validate any piece of equipment currently carried onboard. These validations are
very important because they build a history of maintenance actions taken on the
equipment in question, and accurate reporting will help NSC track the
reliability and maintenance needs for similar equipment across the fleet. Each
item has a specific Record Identification Number, or RIN, assigned by the ship's
Configuration Data Manager, or CDM.

Deferred Work Notification: A work notification, also known as OPNAV 4790/2K
after the form it used to be written on, is the method used to record work
required or that has been completed on any shipboard system, component, or
surface. The sum of all currently active WNs onboard are collectively referred
to as the Current Ship's Maintenance Project, or CSMP. The WN has a number of
required inputs:
1. Equipment: The specific equipment or space the WN is written about. This
   should be as specific as possible; if it is a job on the TAO console in CIC
   you should not be using the RIN for the RSC console.
2. JCN: The Job Control Number, assigned sequentially when 3MC marks the WN as a
   2-Kilo Job. Becomes the official reference number until the job is closed.
3. Summary: A brief description of the problem. The combination of this block
   and the equipment line should tell you "what happened where" for the issue
   you are reporting.
4. Problem Description: This is where you explain the issue at hand. You should
   use plain English (jargon only where professionally applicable) and be as
   descriptive as possible. This may be what sets up a tech assist or repair
   team visit, so you need to provide an explanation clear enough that it's the
   only briefing necessary to get the aid required.
5. Recommendation: The second half to your description. Again, explain clearly
   and directly what you think would help, whether it's requesting distance
   support, ordering parts, getting another work center to assist, or having a
   flooring team resurface your decking. If you're asking for a pipe, deck, or
   lagging to be replaced, you must give the dimensions required so that the job
   is accurately bid. Without measurements, space repair WNs WILL be rejected.
6. POC: First POC should be the person writing the WN. Second will be the LCPO
   or DLCPO.
7. Safety Related: Any mark other than "BLANK" requires review by command Safety
   Officer.
8. Priority: Always 4 (Desirable) unless there is a CASREP associated with the
   JCN. A Cat 2 CASREP gets Priority 3 (Highly Desirable), Cat 3 - Priority 2.
9. Type Avail: [Source text garbled here] ... required: 4 is your WC and 4B is
   any other WC on the ship. 4B needs an assist WC listed. Any other level will
   be sent off ship for review.

NOTE: When writing a job in AWN characters will auto-capitalize. But when a job
is exported the auto-capitalization will not carry over and many jobs and
comments come out in all lowercase. Therefore, it is highly recommended to
either turn CAPS LOCK ON when entering jobs or write everything in a program
like MS Word and copy it over when complete.

Supporting Illustration: Also known as OPNAV 4790/2L after the official form
that used to be required, now includes any drawing, digital photo, tech pub
figure, or CAD file that supports and illustrates the issues or required
solution for an outside activity. Not currently available in AWN but may be
forwarded to outside activities via email or DoDSafe.

Further information on AWN can be found in the MOM AWN Software manual,
EE660-AK-SUM-010, or in the "Help" tab on AWN.

SKED 3.2
--------
SKED 3.2: The program used for scheduling and accounting for PMS throughout the
Navy. Some of its notable features include:
- True Interval Scheduling, which means that a monthly check is automatically
  scheduled 29 days after it is completed, annual checks are scheduled 364 days
  later, and so on. It can also automatically schedule multiple checks with a
  single trigger, such as entering port or refueling at sea.
- Equipment-based schedules, where each RIN is tied to the maintenance performed
  on it so trends can continue to be monitored throughout the fleet.
- Schedule, Review, and Forecasting Displays. These permit Work Center
  Supervisors to see periodicity markers, man-hour tallies, and other equipment
  details.
- Automated Workflow. Each user has a personalized dashboard that displays
  maintenance assigned to them, spot checks scheduled, and relevant alerts.
- Electronic Line-Outs. Permits WCS to automatically tailor their MIPs and MRCs
  without drawing lines and electronically route them for approval by LCPO/DIVO.
- Tailored Force Revision. Allows the NSLC to send each command a revision that
  already includes their MIPs and Line-outs, minimizing the work required by
  each Work Center to install the new FR each quarter.

SKED 3.2 has a number of View Tabs, which each perform a different function.
Some tabs are not available to all users, depending on permissions. The views
are: My Tasks, Workcenter, PMS Viewer, FBR, SPMIG, Spot Checks, Training, LOEP
Manager, Events, Reports, and Admin. The View Tabs can also be accessed through
the "View" drop-down menu on the Menu Bar.

The following are the views relevant to the Maintenance Person.

My Tasks View: The default view when you log in to SKED. This is tailored to
your role in SKED and may include a Summary view displaying metrics for Work
Centers under your authority. All hands will have a Tasks view displaying all
checks assigned in the next two weeks, any spot checks scheduled, PMS alerts,
weekly closeouts, and revision/lineout approvals, as they apply. You can also
change your password and manage your CAC login in the "My Tasks" drop-down menu.

Workcenter View: This is the most commonly used view in normal SKED operation.
When first opened, you must select a work center to view. Only one work center
can be viewed at a time. There are several panels available in Workcenter View:
- Schedule Display. Lists all maintenance tasks for the selected Work Center in
  the active quarter. Default view is broken up by week, but can also view by
  equipment or crew. Functions available include add/delete/reschedule check,
  assign crew, mark complete/lost, and enter Check Note.
- Review Display. Allows the user to view maintenance history. List is separated
  by MIP and then equipment groups. In this view you can see the last time each
  MRC was completed and the date range available for the next occurrence.
- Forecast Display. Shows upcoming checks for the quarter, listed by equipment
  or by crew member. Can also be adjusted to view Man Hours or Elapsed Time for
  each week's maintenance.
- Situational Display. Shows situational maintenance ("R" checks) listed by
  triggering event. The "Events" view can be used to set local states or
  triggers for multiple pieces of equipment simultaneously, and the "Custom
  Events" view can be used to build local triggers for multiple checks.
- IEM Display. Shows equipment previously or currently in IEM broken up by MIP.
  This is the view used by the Department Head to set equipment in IEM status.
- Journal Display. Shows every action taken in the work center, which crew
  member did it and the date/time the action occurred. Listed by category or
  date. This is the tool used by inspectors to verify each crew member is
  performing their designated role and not acting outside their official
  capacity.
- PMS Documents Display. Lists all MIPs and MRCs carried in the WC. From here
  you can print current MRCs and MIPs with all locally added updates, lineouts
  and LLs already inserted.

FBR View: Allows personnel to enter Feedback Reports (FBRs) for technical
clarification or administrative update. FBRs are generated via Feedback Wizard.
Verbiage should be similar to a job. Write clearly and directly in plain
language to best explain the problem and any recommended solution. Once the FBR
is submitted, the chain of command has 3-5 calendar days to approve and 3MC must
forward off ship by the 7th day after generation. Urgent FBRs must have naval
message DTG included in the request.

SPMIG View: Searchable listing of all TPMTE associated to any listed
maintenance. Excellent resource to verify correct materials or identify NIIN for
RPPO. Includes SCAT Codes for test equipment.

Training View: Provides training videos for SKED processes.

Reports View: Allows generation of reports for print or export, including 13
Week Reports or Forecasting reports for parts or HAZMAT.

Further information available in SKED 3.2 help file.
